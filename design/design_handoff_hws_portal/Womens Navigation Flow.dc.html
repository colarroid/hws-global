Navigation model

How she moves, and what she never loses

The approved path

1.0 need → 2.0 where → 3.0 situation → 4.0 working it out → 5.0 next steps → the organisation's own site. From 5.0: the Refine chip → 6.0; the header → 7.0; nothing found → 8.0. Save on any card → 9.0 → 10.0, first time only → 11.0 her list → 12.0 settings.

Still to approve

The fourth question. All three question screens are labelled “of 4”, but no fourth screen is in the approved set. Either it exists and needs drawing, or the counters should read “of 3”. The landing screen, the passcode entry screen, the empty saved list, and the deadline reminder email — each one is reachable from an approved screen, so the flow has gaps without them. The service detail page behind “Learn more”, and the handover to the organisation's site. Error and offline states, sensitive-category handling and its exit-control explainer, helper mode for support workers, cold arrivals from a search engine, and reading and access controls (text size, contrast, language, low data).

Rules that hold everywhere

Back is always one step, labelled with where it goes (“Change answers”, “Back to results”), and never loses what she typed. Refine happens in place. Answer chips on 5.0 are removable; removing one re-runs the search and keeps her scroll position. Widen is additive and reversible, with a banner naming the current scope and a one-tap return. Start over is always available, confirms once, and is never the only way forward. Close sits in the same place on every screen in the search flow, and closes straight away. Saving never requires an account. The star works immediately for the session; the account only makes the list outlive the window. Searching, reading and applying never ask her to sign in. State lives in the URL, so Back, refresh, sharing and printing all work, and the browser's own back button behaves. Every target 44px or larger. Keyboard order: skip link, Back, Close, then content.

Where the flow risks losing her

1.0 — the blank box. Free text is the whole promise of “tell us what you need”, and also the point where digital confidence bites. Suggestions must be visible before she types. 3.0 — twelve chips, and no Skip. Any hint of being sorted into a category loses trust. With the Skip button removed, Next has to be visibly usable with nothing selected. 5.0 — card weight. Eleven pieces of information per card is a lot of screen. If scroll fatigue shows in testing, collapse the lower half of the cards below Start here — never Start here itself, and never behind a login. 8.0 — nothing matched. The single highest-risk screen. If it reads as failure she won't return, and she'll tell other women. Every widening we offer must be pre-counted so it is never another dead end. 9.0 — the only ask in the flow. Every other screen gives without requesting. If the sign-in prompt drifts earlier — onto results, or before she has saved anything — the no-login promise reads as a bait and switch. 10.0 — three fields we don't need. The profile step asks for a name and a phone number in a flow that promised to ask for nothing, and its own body copy still says “no name, no postcode”. The phone number has no use in Phase Two. Data freshness. The verified stamp is the trust mechanism. Stale dates undo the ranking work, so a re-check cadence and a “something wrong?” reporting loop need an owner before launch.
